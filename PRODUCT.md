# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Staff of a single restaurant (Levantine concept, "Saffron & Cedar") using an internal operations system.

- **Manager** — the primary daily user. Works on a desktop/laptop in a back office: focused console sessions reviewing reservations, adjusting the floor plan, managing guest profiles, staff accounts, and the restaurant's branding.
- **Hostess** — works the door during service: arrivals, seating, walk-ins, unit booking.
- Both sign in with staff/PIN auth. No public-facing screens today; a guest-facing booking side is a **future plan only** and does not shape current work.

## Product Purpose

Run the restaurant's service operation end to end: today's floor plan and unit states, reservation oversight and run sheet, guest profiles and tags, staff accounts, and the restaurant's own branding. Success looks like the manager answering "what's happening tonight and what needs my attention?" within seconds, and service staff seating guests without friction.

## Brand Personality

**Warm, grounded, precise.** The interface should feel like the restaurant itself — dark, calm surfaces with a single saffron-gold accent used deliberately — but behave like a tool: quiet, fast, legible. Confidence comes from clarity, not ornament.

## Anti-references

- **Fine-dining theatrics** — gold everywhere, ornate serif flourishes, decorative gradients, luxury-brand cosplay. Restraint is the point; the palette already carries the identity.

## Design Principles

1. **Clarity is the luxury** — one deliberate accent, high contrast, nothing decorative competing with data.
2. **Service-speed reading** — every screen answers the urgent question (what's happening now) in one glance; hierarchy does the work before the user reads a word.
3. **Desktop-first density** — the manager's console is a focused desktop session; use the space for precise, scannable information rather than spreading for its own sake.
4. **Honest data** — empty and zero states are real and common (a restaurant starts with zero reservations); they must look intentional, never broken or faked.
5. **The brand whispers** — the restaurant's warm identity lives in the palette, type weight, and small details, never in themed decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA: body text ≥ 4.5:1 contrast against its background, large text ≥ 3:1, visible focus states, keyboard-reachable controls. Gold-on-dark and muted-gray-on-dark combinations must be verified against AA thresholds. Generous click/touch targets — staff use these screens standing and mid-conversation.
